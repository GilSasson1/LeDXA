# Supplementary data tables

Machine-readable data for the Supplementary Tables of the LeDXA manuscript. One file per
table, named by its Supplementary Table number. Each is the **complete** table: every row
and every value in its own column, including unadjusted *P* values and the raw analysis
identifiers, where the typeset appendix may show a summary.

Generated by `supplement/build_supplementary_tables.py` — do not edit by hand.

The legends below are the manuscript's appendix legends. Where one says a table is
*summarised*, that describes the typeset appendix version — the file here is always the
complete table.

### Supplementary Table 1 — Baseline characteristics of the study populations
`TableS1_cohort_characteristics.csv`

Characteristics of the analytic samples in the internal (HPP) and external (UK Biobank) cohorts - participants with complete age, sex and BMI and a non-degraded whole-body scan (Methods, 'Exclusions and analytic samples'). Continuous variables are mean ± s.d. (range); sex is n (%). No diagnosis- or medication-based exclusions were applied.

### Supplementary Table 2 — HPP prevalent-disease classification (AUROC)
`TableS2_hpp_disease_auroc.csv`

Mean AUROC ± s.e.m. over 10 subject-level splits for four covariate-adjusted arms (covariates only; LeDXA; DINOv3; DXA-tabular) across 37 chronic self-reported HPP conditions with ≥100 cases (DXA-direct, sex-specific and non-chronic endpoints excluded). All arms use 10 seeds. Markers denote that LeDXA significantly exceeds that arm: *adj. P < 0.05, **adj. P < 0.01 (two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank across seeds, Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted). LeDXA is the reference arm and never carries a marker. Exact P values, including the unadjusted values and the comparisons between the non-LeDXA arms, are in the CSV export.

### Supplementary Table 3 — UK Biobank prevalent-disease classification (AUROC)
`TableS3_ukbb_disease_auroc.csv`

As Supplementary Table 2, for the 28 chronic ICD-10 conditions in the external UK Biobank cohort (osteoporosis excluded as DXA-direct; cohort size 25,945; 10 seeds per arm). Markers denote that LeDXA significantly exceeds that arm: *adj. P < 0.05, **adj. P < 0.01 (two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank across seeds, Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted). LeDXA is the reference arm and never carries a marker. Exact P values, including the unadjusted values and the comparisons between the non-LeDXA arms, are in the CSV export. Prevalence and the corresponding area under the precision-recall curve for every arm are in the CSV export.

### Supplementary Table 4 — HPP disease organ-system grouping
`TableS4_hpp_disease_organ_systems.csv`

Every diagnosis tracked in the HPP DXA cohort (8,820 participants with a usable whole-body scan), its organ-system assignment from the LabData medical-conditions mapping, and the number of participants reporting it at baseline. System total (n) is the participant count for the whole system, so it exceeds any single diagnosis and is not the sum of the rows (participants may report several). The CSV export additionally flags which diagnoses are analysed individually in Supplementary Table 2. Of the 21 system labels, 14 hold at least one individually analysed condition. **Summarised here**: those 14 systems; the other seven, which contain no individually analysed condition, are in the data file only. Excluding the acute/procedural Surgery group from the 14 leaves the 13 organ-system endpoints evaluated in Extended Data Fig. 3 (Cardiovascular, Dermatology, ENT, Endocrinology, Gastro, Hematological, Metabolic, Neurologic, Orthopedic, Pulmonology, Rheumatology, Sleep, Urology). Allergy is the only diagnosis the source maps to two systems (Dermatology and Immunology); it is assigned here to Dermatology, the primary system the analysis uses, which is why Immunology retains no analysed condition, and its case count is the union across both systems and so exceeds the Dermatology total.

### Supplementary Table 5 — UK Biobank incident-disease Cox discrimination (C-index)
`TableS5_ukbb_incident_disease_cindex.csv`

Mean Harrell's C-index ± s.e.m. per model arm (LeDXA, DINOv3, DXA-tabular, covariates only) across 20 incident-disease and mortality endpoints in UK Biobank (reproduces Fig. 3). Endpoints comprise 22,481-25,945 participants each, with a median follow-up of 3.7-4.3 years. Markers denote that LeDXA significantly exceeds that arm: *adj. P < 0.05, **adj. P < 0.01 (two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank across seeds, Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted). LeDXA is the reference arm and never carries a marker. Exact P values, including the unadjusted values and the comparisons between the non-LeDXA arms, are in the CSV export. Per-endpoint participant counts, follow-up times and organ-system categories are in the CSV export.

### Supplementary Table 6 — LeDXA embedding GWAS hits (genome-wide and suggestive)
`TableS6_gwas_hits.csv`

Genome-wide-significant (P < 5×10⁻⁸; n = 254) and suggestive (5×10⁻⁸ ≤ P < 1×10⁻⁵; n = 635) hits from the LeDXA embedding GWAS (20 principal components, UK Biobank), reported as the minimum-P association across PCs; 889 rows. Columns: rsID, locus (chromosome:position, GRCh37), effect/other allele, BETA ± s.e.m., P, best-associated principal component, nearest gene. PCs are numbered as in the per-PC summary statistics (PC0-PC19); an em dash for nearest gene denotes an intergenic hit. **Summarised here**: one row per principal component, giving its hit counts and its strongest association. The complete SNP-level table (889 rows, with chromosome, position, both alleles, BETA ± s.e.m. and significance tier as separate columns) is provided as the separate file **TableS6_gwas_hits.csv**.

### Supplementary Table 7 — Paired biological-age-gap change by ATC-3 medication class and sex
`TableS7_medication_bioage_gap.csv`

Change in the LeDXA biological-age gap (follow-up minus baseline) after treatment initiation, analysed separately in women and men (Fig. 5h). Participants contribute to a class if their first reported use of it fell strictly between their baseline and follow-up DXA scans. 23 ATC level-3 classes plus a pooled 'Other' bucket were analysed; the 37 class × sex combinations retaining more than 10 paired participants are reported, covering 22 of those classes (beta blocking agents, C07A, reached n > 10 in neither sex). Class names are the official ATC level-3 names. Those 37 combinations form the multiple-testing family. P values are from two-sided one-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank tests against a null of no change, Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted across those 37 combinations. Rows are ordered by unadjusted P. Mean gap before and after initiation, and the unadjusted P values, are in the CSV export.

### Supplementary Table 8 — Female embedding-cluster matched phenotypic differences
`TableS8_cluster_phenotypes.csv`

Phenotypic comparisons between Cluster B and Cluster A in the age- and BMI-matched female cohort (1,035 matched pairs): Cohen's d (Cluster B minus A) with its 95% confidence interval, and Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted P values. **Summarised here**: within each physiological system, the three features with the smallest adjusted P among those reaching adj. P < 0.05, with the number significant in that system given alongside; 255 features were significant across 13 systems. All 1,794 features tested across 22 systems - **TableS8_cluster_phenotypes.csv** provides all 255 significant features in full - unadjusted P values, confidence intervals, the raw analysis identifier for each feature and the two cluster sizes as separate columns. Adjusted P values were computed over all 1,794 features tested across 22 systems, which is the multiple-testing family even though only the significant subset is tabulated. Cluster sizes vary by feature because of differing missingness.

### Supplementary Table 9 — Female embedding-cluster multi-omics hits
`TableS9_cluster_multi_omics.csv`

Differentially abundant features across plasma proteomics (Olink), NMR metabolomics, transcriptomics and the gut microbiome, comparing Cluster B with Cluster A in the matched female cohort, at adj. P < 0.10; a positive mean difference means the feature is higher in Cluster B. Differences are on each platform's native scale, stated alongside the layer: Olink values are on a log₂ scale, a log₂ scale, so the proteomic differences are log₂ fold-changes; NMR values are in the platform's concentration units and microbiome values are relative abundances, which is why their magnitudes are not comparable across layers. Group sizes differ by platform because omics coverage of the matched cohort differs. Neither confidence intervals nor per-group means and standard deviations are available from the differential-abundance outputs, which record only the difference, its P value and the group sizes. Five features reach adj. P < 0.05: MYL3, LRIG1 and ITGB6 (proteomics) and creatinine and GlycA (NMR). One transcript, F8A1, met the threshold but was investigated and excluded as an artefact, so no transcriptomic hit is reported. The CSV export additionally carries the unadjusted P values and a confidence tier (strong, adj. P < 0.05; discovery, adj. P < 0.10).

### Supplementary Table 10 — DXA tabular feature dictionary (HPP and UK Biobank)
`TableS10_dxa_feature_dictionary.csv`

The scanner-derived DXA measurements used by the DXA-tabular baseline (Methods), listed by cohort with measurement category. 318 features in HPP and 117 in UK Biobank (435 total). Categories are assigned from the measurement type so that the two cohorts are directly comparable: bone area and geometry, bone mineral content (BMC), bone mineral density (BMD) and body composition. BMC (g) and BMD (g cm⁻²) are distinct quantities reported at the same skeletal sites, BMD being mineral content divided by projected area, so the three bone categories are not redundant. Two HPP fields are acquisition-mode indicators rather than phenotypes: the scanner records a binary dose setting (0.4 or 0.8 µSv) that it raises for thicker participants, so they behave as a coarse body-size flag. They are retained in the DXA-tabular feature set and listed separately here. Columns: feature index, cohort, scanner field name, display label, measurement category. **Summarised here**: feature counts by measurement category and cohort. The feature-level dictionary is provided as the separate file **TableS10_dxa_feature_dictionary.csv**.

## Plotting-pipeline files (not supplementary tables)

`table_2_hpp_disease.csv` and `table_3_ukbb_disease.csv` are the internal-schema inputs that
`plotting/fig2_heatmap.py` reads. They hold the same statistics as Supplementary Tables 2 and 3
but keep the raw analysis column names and the abbreviated disease labels used as figure axis
ticks, so the figure code is not coupled to publication formatting. For any use other than
regenerating Figure 2, read the `TableS*.csv` files above.

`plotting/fig3_cox.py` writes `table_5_incident_disease.csv` here as a by-product; it is
git-ignored, and its published form is `TableS5_ukbb_incident_disease_cindex.csv`.
